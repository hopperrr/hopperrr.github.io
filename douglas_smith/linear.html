The 19th Century Grain Trade

A Linear Programming Transportation Model (Circa 1850)

Historical Context: In the mid-19th century, the American wheat trade was revolutionized by the Erie Canal, the expansion of the B&O Railroad, and Mississippi river steamboats. Chicago emerged as the great Midwestern hub, while New Orleans controlled the river trade. This tool uses Linear Programming to calculate the absolute cheapest way to ship wheat from Western/Southern markets to the booming Eastern seaboard cities, factoring in the varying costs of canals, rails, and coastal shipping.
